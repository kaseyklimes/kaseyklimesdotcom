# Crop assessment

Implementation update: approved crops applied to Ladies (22% top, 5% left), Bishop climber and observer (33% right), Barcelona candy stall (25% top, 12% right), and Bishop parked vehicles (18% top). New `-crop-v1.jpg` assets are referenced by the site. The Mountains 2011 crop was rejected and its original remains in use. The assessment below records the original proposals.

September 13, 2026. Assessment of the 41 remaining photographs after removing the Bishop packing photo from the series. These are proposed starting points based on visual inspection, not applied or rendered crop comparisons. Percentages refer to the original frame's width or height removed from the stated edge, not percentages of the sky or another subject. Final boundaries should be adjusted visually. No image assets were changed.

Other plausible experiments:

- **Daphne:** remove about 15–20% from the left and 8–12% from the bottom. Keep the supporting hands and both yellow smiley faces. This makes the visual rhyme clearer, but the bright skin and competing background remain.
- **Crowd with phones:** trim 15–20% from the top, leaving generous clearance over the highest phone. This increases immediacy, but the original's empty sky helps make the absent spectacle felt. I would compare both rather than assume tighter is better.
- **Cherry-blossom finch:** a moderate crop around the central bird would help it read at small sizes. Preserve flowers and space in the direction it faces. Aggressive enlargement magnifies softness and the obstructing branch, so the possible improvement has a ceiling.
- **Azores dolphin:** a tighter water-only frame could remove the bright coastline and enlarge the action. Keep more space in front of the dolphin, toward the left. It still cannot restore detail in the bright body; this would improve emphasis without making it a leading image.
- **Bishop boulders:** a small reduction in sky could help, but preserve generous clearance above the standing figure. Avoid sacrificing secondary people at the lower right to make the composition superficially simpler.

I would be cautious about cropping Frenchy's or the Viñales cigar image. Removing the yellow box and poles from Frenchy's would also sacrifice much of the truck; removing the bottle in Viñales would bring the boundary uncomfortably close to the hands and smoke. Their distractions overlap with useful context, so there is no clean, obviously superior crop.

I would preserve the existing framing of La Flor, the abandoned Langeland room, Papelería, the Azores swimming rocks, roller hockey, and the Barcelona window. Their peripheral details and spatial relationships are central to their strength. Several other pictures could tolerate tiny trims, but I do not see a material improvement worth prioritizing.

The first five proposals are the most promising practical changes. The remaining proposals are more dependent on whether the desired effect is graphic concentration, atmosphere, or contextual storytelling.
